## `<Klipp>` and `KlippCore`

`<Klipp>` is both the provider and the driver - it must be mounted inside `<Canvas>`, since it drives
itself via its own `useFrame`. Every frame it:

1. Updates every mounted, `active` `<VirtualCamera>` (its own Body→Aim→Extension→Noise pipeline, see below).
2. Calls `KlippCore.tick(dt)`, which arbitrates priority and blends toward the winner.
3. Writes the result onto the real camera.

`KlippCore` is the piece that actually owns this - a plain class with no React dependency, reachable via
`useKlippCore()` if you need it directly. It keeps a registry of every candidate camera and decides which
one wins:

- **Highest `priority` wins.** Ties go to whichever camera was live most recently, not registration order.
- **A camera that stops winning stays live until its own outgoing blend finishes** - switching priority
  back and forth mid-blend doesn't cut, it re-targets the blend already in flight
  (*mid-blend interruption*).

## `<VirtualCamera>`

A `<VirtualCamera>` is a candidate, not necessarily what's on screen - `name` and `priority` are what
`KlippCore` arbitrates on, and `active` (default `true`) controls whether it's a candidate **at all**,
independent of `priority`. `active={false}` fully unregisters it - its Body/Aim/Noise don't even run, not
just "loses arbitration."

```tsx
<VirtualCamera name="follow-cam" priority={10} active={true}>
  {/* one Body, one Aim, any number of Extension/Noise */}
</VirtualCamera>
```

## The pipeline: Body → Aim → Extension → Noise

Each `<VirtualCamera>` owns one mutable `CameraState` (see below) that its children write into, always in
this order:

1. **Body** computes **position**. At most one per `<VirtualCamera>` - `HardLockToTarget`, `Follow`,
   `PositionComposer`, or [`CameraControls`](/camera-controls)*.
2. **Aim** computes **rotation**, reading whatever position Body just wrote. At most one -
   `HardLookAt`, `RotateWithFollowTarget`, or `RotationComposer`.
3. **Extension** runs after Body+Aim, since it needs the shot's final orientation - `GroupFraming` dollies
   along the camera's own current view axis, `Lens` overrides `fov`/`near`/`far`. Unlike Body/Aim, this
   slot **stacks**: mount as many as you need.
4. **Noise** runs last, adding shake on top of an already-correctly-framed shot instead of one an
   Extension might still adjust out from under it. Also a stacking slot.

Body never touches rotation and Aim never touches position - they're deliberately independent, so you can
follow one target with one damping curve while looking at a completely different one.

\* `CameraControls` is the one deliberate exception: orbiting couples position and rotation inherently
(an orbit camera always looks at its target), so it writes both. It's an adapter around the
[`camera-controls`](https://github.com/yomotsu/camera-controls) library for user-driven input, not one of
klipp's own composable pieces - see its [own page](/camera-controls) rather than treating it as just
another Body.

## `CameraState`

The value threaded through that whole pipeline is a single, plain `CameraState` object: `position`,
`quaternion`, `fov`, `near`, `far`, `viewOffset`, plus the Body's `target`/Aim's `lookAtTarget` (used by
blend hints, see [Blend Hints](/blending/blend-hints)). It's zero-allocation by convention - every
Body/Aim/Extension/Noise mutates the `out` parameter it's given in place rather than returning a new
object, safe to call every frame without generating garbage.

## Blending and priority arbitration

Only one camera is ever actually **live** - what `<Klipp>` currently writes onto the real camera. When
arbitration picks a new winner, `KlippCore` blends from the previous live `CameraState` to the new one,
not an instant cut - either a [named curve](/blending/blend-curves) over a fixed duration, or `damping`
(seconds), which approaches the new camera asymptotically instead of finishing at a fixed pace, the same
spring as every other damped field in klipp. `defaultBlend` on `<Klipp>` sets this globally;
[Custom Blends](/blending/custom-blends) override it for specific from→to camera pairs.

## `frameloop="demand"` and re-renders

If your `<Canvas>` uses `frameloop="demand"`, klipp keeps the loop alive on its own **as long as
something is actually changing** frame to frame - a moving target, an in-progress blend, damping still
catching up. But a mutation that doesn't go through React or `useFrame` (an imperative
`target.position.set(...)` in a click handler, for example) won't wake the loop by itself. Call
`invalidate()` right after a mutation like that, or it'll sit un-rendered until something else happens to
request a frame.
